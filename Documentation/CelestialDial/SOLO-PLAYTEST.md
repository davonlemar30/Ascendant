# Celestial Dial solo playtest checklist

This is for you to play the game once, start to finish, and write down what happened. You are the only tester. What you decide at the end is the decision.

Play here: https://davonlemar30.github.io/Ascendant/  ·  Write your decision here: https://app.clickup.com/t/86bbyd0vw

Check a box only if you saw it happen. An empty box means you did not test it. Short notes are good. What matters is what your hand did, not whether it sounded fun.

One word to know: the wheel moves in clicks, and each click is one sign. (We sometimes call a click a "detent" or "notch." Same thing.)

## Before you start

This checks that you are set up the way the test expects.

- [ ] The "Reduced motion" button at the bottom of the screen says "off" for your first play.

## The opening

This checks that the screens before the wheel work and do not trap you.

- [ ] The game opens in the dark and asks "WHO ARE YOU?" Type a name or leave it empty. Continue works either way.
- [ ] It asks "Do you know when you were born?" and shows three choices. Continue stays off until you pick one.
- [ ] Pick one of the first two choices. A note says chart entry is not in this build and a teaching sign will be used. Pick "I don't know" instead and there is no note. All three lead to the same practice sign, Taurus.
- [ ] A white flash, then a big room: dust, covered furniture, sealed doors, one candle. Caspar says hello and points you to the Zodiac Wing. Continue takes you there.

## Your first touch of the wheel

This checks what your hand does before anyone explains anything.

- [ ] Before reading anything, what did your thumb try first: drag the wheel, tap a sign, or tap a button? ___
- [ ] Press Next once. Did the wheel move the way you expected? Any feeling it went the wrong way? ___
- [ ] The first time you drag, it feels the same as every other drag. It is not heavier.
- [ ] The white bracket sits around exactly one sign when the wheel stops. Just sitting there does not count as an answer.

## The Earth family, with Caspar's help

This checks that the wheel feels right and that the first family teaches the pattern.

- [ ] Caspar says Taurus is a practice sign that stands in for your own.
- [ ] Drag four clicks in one thumb sweep. Every click feels the same. The fourth click is not special.
- [ ] Let go between two signs. The wheel settles on the closest sign, fast, with no bounce.
- [ ] Flick the wheel. It may coast one extra click, never more.
- [ ] The screen says "Framed: " and the sign name. Nothing is sent until you press Keeper's Seal.
- [ ] Press Seal on Virgo. Virgo lights up and shows "Earth." The wheel goes back to Aries by itself, quietly. Then it moves to Virgo by itself for the next problem, with no clicks counted.
- [ ] Finish with Capricorn. A line joins the three Earth signs. The game does not use any new astrology words here.

## The Fire family, on your own

This checks that you can use the pattern with less help.

- [ ] The wheel moves to Aries by itself with no clicks counted. Caspar says less. The Count button is still there, and it never tells you the answer.
- [ ] Use only Next and Previous, no dragging. Press Next five times, Previous once, then Seal. The game says it is right, and it counts as your own work.
- [ ] For the next Fire problem, tap the sign you want. It moves under the bracket but nothing is sent yet. Press Seal and it is accepted.
- [ ] Six signs are lit and six are dark. Two families have lines. The Keeper Key message shows once. The game never uses the words "Retained," "Sealed," or "restored."
- [ ] The game offers one more problem, the Air family. Did you want to take it? Did you keep turning the wheel when nothing asked you to? ___

## The Keeper Key and the ending

This checks the two reward moments, the small one at the wheel and the big one in the Chamber.

- [ ] After the sixth sign lights, Caspar says "Two of four. The rest will wait for you." Then the wheel splits along its middle and a Keeper Key rises out. Caspar says "Aah... the Library stirs." Did the split look like a reveal, or like something breaking? ___
- [ ] "Keeper Key: 1" shows at the top. The marks on the floor get brighter and the candle lights. A Continue button appears. The extra Air problem is still offered too.
- [ ] Back in the big room, Caspar says to come with him. Continue takes you to the Chamber.
- [ ] The Chamber shows seven sealed Books with three locks each, and a dark chandelier. Press "Insert the Key." One lock lights up. The chandelier flickers twice, then every candle lights. The round mechanism turns a little. Caspar says "So he was right." Then "End of prototype." Did the ending land? ___

## Getting it wrong on purpose

This checks how the game helps when you make mistakes. Start the game over for this part.

- [ ] Get a Seal wrong once. An × shows by the sign. The wheel stays where you put it. Caspar says "Not that one. Count your steps again." The screen says "Help level 1."
- [ ] Get the same problem wrong again. Caspar tells you the rule: "Start at [sign]. Count each sign after it: one, two, three, four." The screen says "Help level 2." Pressing Count does not change that. Flicking no longer coasts.
- [ ] Get it wrong a third time. Caspar shows you one, counting the signs out loud on the wheel. Then you get a new problem from a different sign in the same family, back at "Help level 0."
- [ ] Do that whole thing again: three wrong Seals, Caspar shows you one, new sign. And again. After the third round, the game stops and says no Key yet. It does not give you a fourth round, even with a new sign.
- [ ] If Caspar had to tell you the rule or show you, that answer does not count toward the Key. Only the new problem you solve on your own can count.

## Other ways to play, without dragging

This checks that every way of moving the wheel gets the same answer.

- [ ] On a computer: the left and right arrow keys move one click. Tab moves between buttons. Enter or Space presses the button you are on. Only Seal sends your answer.
- [ ] With VoiceOver on your iPhone (or TalkBack on Android): each sign is read out with its name, its place from 1 to 12, whether it is framed, and its element if it is lit. When you move, it reads which sign is framed and the count.
- [ ] Turn "Reduced motion" on. The wheel does not slide or coast, there is no white flash, no split animation, no chandelier flicker, and you can still finish everything.
- [ ] Dragging, Next and Previous, tapping a sign, and arrow keys all get the same answer accepted.

## Does it fit on the phone?

This checks that nothing is cut off or too small.

- [ ] Nothing needs scrolling on any screen. The wheel is the biggest thing on its screen. Seal is the biggest button.
- [ ] On a smaller phone (or a small window, 360 wide by 800 tall), still nothing needs scrolling, and the signs and the Count button are easy to tap.
- [ ] Every state is shown with words or shapes, not only colors. If you could not see color, you could still tell what is lit, what is framed, and what was wrong.

## Dev notes (skip if you're just playing)

- Reference sizes: 390 × 844 logical pixels, and it must fit at 360 × 800. On desktop Chrome, use the device toolbar to check the smaller size.
- The event log prints to the browser console with the prefix `CelestialDial`. Optional.
- First-drag resistance is off by default; `DialView.firstDragResistance` re-enables it for comparison. Inertia is `DialView.inertiaEnabled`.
- Known and not a defect: automated pointer input in the very first frame after a phase transition is ignored; not reachable by a human.
- Scripted checks (mechanical, Play Mode, browser) live in `VALIDATION.md`. They are not this playtest.
